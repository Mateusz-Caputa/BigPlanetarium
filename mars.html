Mars

Terrestrial Planet • 4th from the Sun

Mars is the fourth planet from the Sun and the second-smallest in the Solar System. Its striking rust-red colour comes from iron oxide — rust — on its surface. Despite being far smaller than Earth, Mars is home to the largest volcano and the longest canyon system in the entire Solar System.

Overview

Mars has a thin atmosphere composed mostly of carbon dioxide (95%), with traces of nitrogen and argon. Its surface pressure is less than 1% of Earth's, meaning liquid water cannot exist there today — though water ice sits at the poles and evidence of ancient rivers and lakes shows it once flowed freely.

Olympus Mons stands 21.9 km above the surrounding plains — nearly three times the height of Mount Everest — making it the tallest volcano known in the Solar System. Running roughly along the Martian equator, Valles Marineris is a canyon system over 4,000 km long and up to 7 km deep; it would stretch across the entire United States.

Mars has two small moons, Phobos and Deimos, believed to be captured asteroids. A Martian day (a "sol") lasts 24 hours and 37 minutes — remarkably close to an Earth day — but a Martian year is 687 Earth days long.

Exploration

Mars is the most-explored planet beyond Earth, with more than 50 missions launched since the 1960s.

  • Mariner 4 (1965) — Returned the first close-up images of Mars, revealing a barren, crater-covered surface.
  • Viking 1 & 2 (1976) — NASA's first successful landers; conducted soil experiments to test for signs of microbial life.
  • Mars Pathfinder (1997) — Deployed Sojourner, the first rover to operate on another planet.
  • Spirit & Opportunity (2004) — Twin rovers that found compelling evidence of ancient water activity.
  • Curiosity (2012–present) — A car-sized rover still active in Gale Crater, studying the planet's past habitability.
  • Perseverance (2021–present) — Collecting rock core samples for future return to Earth and testing oxygen production from Martian air.
  • Ingenuity (2021–2024) — A small helicopter that proved powered flight is possible in Mars's thin atmosphere.
