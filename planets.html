Planets of the Solar System

Our Solar System is home to eight remarkable planets grouped into three families: rocky terrestrial worlds, colossal gas giants, and distant ice giants. Use the filters below to explore each family.

  • Mercury

    Terrestrial

    The smallest planet and closest to the Sun. With no atmosphere to hold heat, temperatures swing from 430°C at noon to −180°C at night.

  • Venus

    Terrestrial

    The hottest planet in the Solar System despite not being the closest to the Sun. Its thick atmosphere traps heat in a runaway greenhouse effect.

  • Earth

    Terrestrial

    Our home — the only planet known to support life. Liquid water, a protective magnetic field, and a breathable atmosphere make Earth uniquely habitable.

  • Mars

    Terrestrial

    The Red Planet is home to Olympus Mons — the tallest volcano in the Solar System — and a canyon network that dwarfs the Grand Canyon.

    Explore Mars

  • Jupiter

    Gas giant

    The largest planet in the Solar System. Jupiter's Great Red Spot is a storm that has raged for at least 350 years, wider than planet Earth itself.

  • Saturn

    Gas giant

    Famous for its stunning ring system, Saturn is made mostly of hydrogen and helium. Its rings span hundreds of thousands of kilometres yet are only ~10 metres thick.

  • Uranus

    Ice giant

    Uranus rotates on its side — likely the result of a massive ancient collision. Its blue-green colour comes from methane absorbing red light in its atmosphere.

  • Neptune

    Ice giant

    The furthest planet from the Sun, Neptune has the fastest winds in the Solar System — reaching over 2,000 km/h. One orbit takes 165 Earth years.
